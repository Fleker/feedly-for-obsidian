Feature: Interview with Bisafans

Bisafans is a German Pokémon fansite which dates back to 2001 when it was created by Robert R. Agular. It shares news and information on the many different Pokémon products and games, with sections of its website dedicated to the anime, manga, trading cards, and more. It also features the BisaBoard, a community forum where fans can gather to discuss a wide range of Pokémon topics. For this interview, I was delighted to speak with Katharina, the current webmaster of Bisafans, who told me all about her website. This interview also includes comments from Basti and Maria, original hosts of BisaCast, the Bisafans podcast.

A selection of logos from Bisafans history, between 2001–2026

Katharina, I am so happy you’ve agreed to this interview with me for Johto Times! Can you please introduce yourself and tell us about your website, Bisafans?

Katharina:

My name is Katharina, and I’m the Webmaster of Bisafans.de. Bisafans is one of the largest Pokémon websites in Germany and has a huge community forum, the BisaBoard, which has nearly 90,000 members. Bisafans covers all topics related to Pokémon, whether it’s anime, manga, games, strategy, trading, fan art, or any other topics.

I first discovered Bisafans through the forum, stayed active in the community, gradually “worked my way up” to become a moderator, then an admin, and have been the proud Webmaster of Bisafans and the BisaBoard since 2014!

Bisafans was first created in 2001 by Robert R. Agular, twenty-five years ago at the time of interview. What can you tell us about those earliest days of Bisafans, such as the history and origins of the community?

Katharina:

While Pokémon is celebrating its 30th anniversary this year, we at Bisafans are celebrating our 25th birthday. Bisafans started out as a small section of the “raffs” website, which was a major private online gaming magazine at the time. It featured news about PC, console, board games, and card games, including Pokémon. At some point, the Pokémon section became so popular and grew so large that the decision was made to give it its own domain, Bisafans.de. That worked out very well, as by fall 2006, the site was even recording around 2 million page views per week. Those were incredible numbers, especially for that time. With the release of Pokémon Diamond and Pearl, Bisafans practically exploded in popularity and had to upgrade to new servers, as the server load at 7 million page views per day could no longer be handled. Since then, Bisafans has been gradually expanded, has added a forum, is active on social media, and has also tried various other areas.

Bisafans layout in March 2001

How did Robert come up with the name Bisafans for his website?

Katharina:

Robert wanted to avoid legal issues and therefore did not want to include “Pokémon” in the domain. He ended up naming the site “Bisafans”, which is a combination of “Bisa” (short for “Bisasam,” the German name for Bulbasaur) and “fans” (because both the site operator and visitors are fans). As far as I know Bisasam was the favorite Pokémon of Robert’s sister at the time, which is why he chose it.

How did you get involved with Bisafans and eventually become its webmaster?

Katharina:

Back then, with Pokémon Diamond & Pearl, I found what was my favorite game at the time. I also wanted to play against other players back then, and if I remember correctly, there was also a Pokémon giveaway at the time that was only available online. So I was really trying to connect my DS to the internet, which is why we bought a special Nintendo Wi-Fi Connector. You could plug it into the computer and then use it to connect the DS to the internet.

However, much to my trouble, the connector didn’t work, and after I bought a second one that also didn’t work, I searched the internet for help. I then came across BisaBoard (I didn’t know about Bisafans back then), signed up there, and asked for help. Not only did I get a lot of help there, but I also stayed active because I loved the online culture and community so much.

Much like on Reddit today, I gradually “worked my way up” and eventually became a moderator, then at some point a global moderator and administrator of the forum. I also became increasingly interested in the website because I wanted to strengthen the connection to the forum in order to attract more new community members. That’s how I got in touch with Robert, who gradually involved me more and more in the site and then, in 2014, when I was 20 years old, handed over practically [all] of the site and full responsibility to me as he wanted to pursue other things. For me, Bisafans is still my favorite hobby, and it’s a real honor to be able to experience new things every day thanks to something so wonderful.

Bisafans layout in May 2001

You were a fan of Pokémon during the earliest days of the series, and I would like to hear more about your experiences growing up with Pokémon during those times. What can you share?

Katharina:

I first became interested in Pokémon in elementary school. I must have been 5 or 6 years old at the time. All the kids in my class knew about Pokémon, and I had a Game Boy with the Red [Version], which I played with my best friends in the schoolyard. Back then, we weren’t allowed to play Game Boy in the school because it was all still very new to the teachers. So we’d secretly find a hiding spot behind some bushes and play together there. Those are my earliest memories of Pokémon.

In the years that followed, I played Pokémon every now and then, but not really “consciously”. For example, I never actually finished my Gold [Version] back then, and it wasn’t until Ruby and Sapphire that my passion for Pokémon really took off. When Diamond and Pearl came out, I was a huge fan and set myself the goal of catching every single Pokémon. I even drove several hundred kilometers with my parents to local giveaways during Pokémon Days in other cities so I could really catch every Pokémon.

Your website covers a wide range of information on Pokémon, including helpful guides and information on the Pokémon video games, animations and trading cards. What are some of your most popular pieces of content that readers value?

Katharina:

The pages that consistently receive the most visits are the Pokédex and the Pokédex with Images, along with the related information on individual Pokémon. At the same time, our guides, walkthroughs, and information pages for new games are also very popular, since I always make it my goal to be the fastest source of information [on] the German-speaking internet. Sometimes I even manage to be one of the first in the world to provide specific information about new games, which makes me very proud. So when new games are released, you can be sure that you’re most likely to find help in German at Bisafans.

In addition, we also post daily news on Pokémon topics from around the world, with a particular focus on Germany, which is also nice to have for German-speaking Pokémon fans.

One of my favourite sections of the website is the BisaComics, which were published between 2005 and 2016. What can you tell us about those?

Katharina:

The comics were drawn back then by a good friend of mine, Maria. She was also a big fan of Bisafans and Pokémon fan, and at the time, she was one of several hosts who presented the BisaCast, a Bisafans podcast. Just for fun, she suggested drawing comics for Bisafans, which were very popular back then and came out regularly for many years.

An archive of the Bisa Comics page

I must also highlight the Pokémon drawing tutorials, which teach people how to draw certain Pokémon. It's not something I have seen on a fansite before. How did that idea come to be?

Katharina:

The idea came through submissions that Robert received for the website via email. He enjoyed publishing fan content on Bisafans, and that’s how the section with the drawing tutorials eventually became a thing. That was also exactly the section I first took over as a moderator back then, and it was my first point of contact between Bisafans and the BisaBoard. We have so many great artists in the community who submitted countless tutorials and voluntarily shared new ideas, which is how the section eventually came into being and grew.

Bisafans has received steady and continuous growth over the years. According to your website, Bisafans was receiving over two million page views per week in Autumn 2006, and the release of Pokémon FireRed & LeafGreen led to your servers being overloaded. What else has contributed to the growth of the community over the years?

Katharina:

I believe the fact that we’ve consistently covered new releases over the years and are posting the latest news and website updates very quickly is the reason why Bisafans has been able to grow so much. I’m often told that people first discovered Bisafans because of a specific page, usually walkthroughs, and have been coming back ever since because they know what to expect. Pokémon is also an international franchise, and some exciting developments, for example, those happening in Japan, are completely unknown on the German market. That’s why we regularly share information about events in Japan or other countries that aren’t just far away but often have a language barrier as well.

Bisafans layout in July 2004

This growth also benefitted your community, not just on social media but on its message board too, with almost 90,000 members at the time of interview. How would you describe your community?

Katharina:

When I think about the community, so many wonderful moments come to mind that I’ve been able to experience thanks to them. Everyone is welcomed warmly on the forum, and everyone can be themselves there. I find it especially fascinating that for every (Pokémon) topic, there’s always an expert who knows it inside and out. If I had to describe it in one word, I’d say “wonderful”.

With such a large audience, it's important that Bisafans publish content accurately and remain credible. What processes do you go through to ensure this is the case?

Katharina:

We publish content only when we are certain that it is accurate. We therefore research primary sources for new stories so that we can report the news accurately, and we often cite those sources as well or translate them from Japanese with one person in our team who can understand the language. As for the guides and walkthroughs, I only publish content on Bisafans that I’ve verified myself. For me, that usually means I have to try things out in the games myself and test them step by step before I post them online. In return, I can be sure that the information is accurate, and at the same time, I can be proud of every single page of mine that goes live.

Bisafans layout in December 2005

BisaCast, the Bisafans podcast, ran for over 100 episodes between July 17th, 2011 and October 19th, 2016. What were some of the highlights from that project?

Katharina:

I personally wasn’t part of the team that produced BisaCast. However, I was a guest and I still remember how incredibly nervous I was back then. It was new for me to talk to others online through a microphone, and it was also weird to hear my own voice.

For this interview, though, I asked two of the original hosts, Basti and Maria, if they could share their highlights with me:

Maria:

Her personal highlights were the events they traveled to. Also she liked to talk about current Pokémon news [and] interviews with other fans. She also could contribute to some shows on stage, which she really enjoyed.

Basti:

Some of my highlights from BisaCast included the episodes where we recorded entire audiobooks ourselves and then immersed ourselves in roles within the Pokémon world. That was always a major highlight, and it was also fun to edit those episodes (even if it was a huuuuuuge amount of work). Then, thanks to BisaCast, we had the chance to travel all across Germany, attend Pokémon Days, share the stage with Oli P. and have fun, and always were able to report from the scene… Those were always wonderful trips and adventures, and we experienced so much. It also gave us a great sense of fulfillment to realize that what we were doing was well-received.

Having interesting guests and being able to visit Nintendo in person was always a highlight, too. It was through Bisacast that we, as Bisafans, were able to build such a strong connection with Nintendo in the first place. We actually got to spend time in their offices and try out new games. In general, being able to test out new Pokémon games and report on them was always a major highlight.

It was also exciting to delve deeper into the world of Pokémon, because we had to gather a lot of information and do a lot of research to compile the details, which was also a lot of fun.

And my personal highlight is that I met my wife through BisaCast. Because without BisaCast, I would never have got to see her in one of her first cosplays in Munich in fall 2011. Without that connection, Maria wouldn’t have joined BisaCast a few months later and expressed a desire to become part of the team. And without BisaCast, Maria and I wouldn’t have been together since February 2, 2012! That was definitely a major highlight for me personally, too.

And finally, there was the opportunity to really get creative in my own way (at least for me), because I’d never had the chance to combine socializing with creating something creative in any way. And for me personally, that was also one of the biggest highlights of BisaCast.

Plus, back then I was able to combine my hobby with my job. I was an announcer on the S-Bahn in Munich and really loved to inform and entertain passengers. Being able to combine that with Pokémon was a highlight. Back when Keno was looking for new people for the cast, there were talks about my application, and that was also a point where I brought up [how] I wanted to turn “my hobby into my career.”

Thanks to my experience with BisaCast, I had the opportunity to perform on stage at Animexx, host the German Cosplay Championships, and take the stage in the anime and manga scene to host events there… BisaCast laid the foundation for all of that for me back then.

Pokémon brings people together; Pokémon opens doors and creates opportunities through the community and the chance for fans to connect with one another. And Pokémon is actually what taught me to read back then. Pokémon Blue was my first Pokémon game, and I wasn’t a good reader at the time. No one read it aloud to me either! So I had to actively engage with it myself.

I learned to read through Pokémon. I met my wife through Pokémon. And thanks to Pokémon, I’ve managed to take the stage nationwide in the anime and manga scene. Having a huge hobby and getting to host events all across the German-speaking world… For me, it’s not just “a BisaCast”. It’s a real “way of life”.

Bisafans layout in December 2008

For over twenty-five years, Bisafans has been a home for many German Pokémon fans, so I am curious to hear what your plans are for the future of the website and community. What are your ambitions for the future of Bisafans?

Katharina:

I really hope for Bisafans and the Bisaboard that we’ll be around for a long time to come and continue to serve as a source of Pokémon information for the German-speaking world. Private forums in particular don’t have it easy because of the many large internet platforms out there, which is why I especially hope that the community will stick with us for a long time and that Bisafans will never come to an end.

Bisafans layout as of November 2026

Johto Times predominantly focuses on English fansites and communities, so this is a great opportunity to hear more about what Pokémon is like for German fans. How would you personally describe the reception to Pokémon there?

Katharina:

I don’t know what it’s like in other countries, but I get the feeling that pretty much everyone in Germany knows about Pokémon by now. About 5 to 10 years ago, things were different. Back then, Pokémon was more of a niche topic that has grown in popularity over time. We even have an online Pokémon Center now that ships to Germany. That makes us one of the few countries with something like this! Since many German-speaking fans also speak English, I feel like we get a lot of international information, but we can then discuss it within the German community with other nuances added.

The Bisacast web page, as it stands in November 2026

We love to share the items and merchandise that our guests have which mean something to them. Do you have anything like that which you can share with us?

Katharina:

Back when I first started getting involved with the Bisafans community, there were often large community gatherings in various cities. For one of these gatherings, many members of the BisaBoard secretly got together to buy a plushie of one of my favorite Pokémon as a thanks for my dedication. I had no idea this was happening, and I was moved to tears when they gave it to me. Of course, I still have that plushie to this day.

Katharina's cherished Blitzle plush, given to her by members of the Bisafans community

At the time of this interview, Pokémon is celebrating its 30-year anniversary. A lot has changed since Robert first created the website, with a tenth generation of Pokémon coming in 2027. On reflection, what are your thoughts of Pokémon and how far it has come?

Katharina:

Pokémon used to be something that only true gaming fans in my circle of friends knew about. Now, Pokémon has become a massive franchise that almost everyone knows. I think it’s great to see how Pokémon has continually reinvented itself over the years and, above all, how it covers so many different aspects. Everyone can decide whether they’d rather play the TCG, play the video games, or create fan art featuring a specific Pokémon. And everyone has their own favorite Pokémon, even if you aren’t a big fan.

Pokémon’s 30th anniversary is themed “What’s Your Favorite?“ and that theme really got me thinking and showed me that every single Pokémon truly has its own fans. I’m really excited to see what the coming years will bring us.

The Bisafans “Bisaboard” forum community, as of November 2026

Katharina, thank you so much for taking the time to tell us all about Bisafans, and about your time with Pokémon so far! Do you have any closing comments to make to our readers, and to fans of Bisafans?

Katharina:

Thanks for inviting me to this interview :) I really enjoyed reflecting on the early days of Bisafans and my journey with the website. It’s always an honor for me to talk about Bisafans, and I’m so happy to be able to share my thoughts and story in this interview!

We are extremely thankful to Katharina and everyone at Bisafans who contributed to this interview. Pokémon fan communities aren’t just limited to English-speaking communities, and it’s great to highlight some of them from other parts of the world. We wish Katharina, and the Bisafans community, all the best of luck and success in the years ahead!
